Calibrate the bias

Twelve synthetic residual pairs represent measured minus reference irradiance factors and signed wind. Each bias has a zero-mean Gaussian prior and a Gaussian residual likelihood. Solar prior and observation SDs are 0.18 and 0.13. Wind SDs are 3.0 and 1.8 m/s.

log α = min(0, log p(candidate | data) − log p(current | data))

Four overdispersed chains run random-walk Metropolis with symmetric Gaussian steps. Each discards 400 warm-up iterations and retains 1,600. The exact conjugate posterior supplies proposal scales and a reference for the sampler. Split R̂ compares variation within and between split chains.

Draw a route forecast

Each race trial resamples one joint bias state with replacement. Stationary AR(1) processes add day-level and leg-level variation to cloud attenuation and signed headwind. This lets nearby legs share weather while each day can differ.

xₜ = ρ xₜ₋₁ + √(1 − ρ²) εₜ, εₜ ∼ N(0, 1)

Day persistence is 0.55 and leg persistence is 0.72. Driving solar is the route's clear-sky scenario times the bounded attenuation factor. Replay telemetry shows each active leg's forecast and switches when that car enters a new leg.

Replay control stops

The native engine timestamps departure, arrival, control release and overnight departure. Every strategy starts at 08:00 on the model clock. Marked controls hold the car for 30 minutes. Route day boundaries park it until the next scheduled 08:00.

The chart connects recorded leg endpoints. The 2025 Cruiser pack is capped at 55 MJ. Overnight external charging is limited by time, charging power and remaining capacity. Replay credits that energy at the next departure.

Rain and road wetness

Station averages set the August wet-day prior along the corridor. The model converts that prior to the time spent on each leg and draws spatially correlated showers. Road wetness builds during rain and persists as the road dries.

W(x + d) = W_eq + [W(x) − W_eq] exp(−d / L)

Rain reduces irradiance. A wet road raises rolling resistance and lowers the cruise cap, even after rain has passed.

Budget charge across the route

The route energy envelope works backward from a terminal charge target. It budgets for the cheapest candidate pace on later legs and credits overnight charging where the pack can hold the required energy.

On the current leg, the controller tests nine paces against that budget with the observed weather. It takes the fastest feasible candidate or falls back to the lowest simulated energy draw.

Integrate gust loads

The gust-aware ECM mode runs the mapped speed cycle at three wind values. Their weighted average captures the effect of wind variability on drag, motor efficiency and capped regeneration.

E[f(W)] ≈ f(μ − √3σ)/6 + 2f(μ)/3 + f(μ + √3σ)/6

Compare racing policies

Each draw feeds the same wind, solar and rolling conditions to three pacing policies. The chosen ECM variant computes aerodynamic drag, rolling loss, grade, kinetic energy, drivetrain losses, regeneration and auxiliary load.

Finish and reserve rates use 95% Wilson intervals. Differences from Steady use paired Newcombe intervals. The intervals quantify Monte Carlo sampling error under the simulated conditions.
